About

Search as archival discovery.

I am a doctoral researcher at the University of Helsinki, working on language models for eighteenth-century intellectual history. My current thesis frames search as a form of archival discovery: a way to identify textual units, evaluate semantic representations, calibrate retrieval, and turn large-scale search into historically meaningful findings.

This work sits between digital humanities and NLP. It uses large digitized archives such as ECCO, dense text representations, expert annotation, and close reading to ask when model similarity becomes usable historical evidence. Before Helsinki, I completed a master's degree in computer science at ShanghaiTech University, where I worked on vision-language reasoning and multimodal machine learning.

Research

Questions I return to

I

Search as archival discovery

Reframing search as a scholarly method for finding historically meaningful passages in large, noisy eighteenth-century archives.

II

Textual units and visibility

Using LLMs to identify hard cases such as Latin fragments embedded in OCR text, where meaningful materials must first become visible.

III

Calibrated semantic retrieval

Evaluating dense representations for implicit reception, paraphrase, lexical gatekeeping, and the limits of model-based historical evidence.

Project

Thesis structure

My thesis develops a search-centered workflow for intellectual history: textual unit identification, semantic representation evaluation, calibrated search, and historical findings with search at scale. The work is part of CASCADE, an EU MSCA Doctoral Network on Computational Analysis of Semantic Change Across Different Environments.
