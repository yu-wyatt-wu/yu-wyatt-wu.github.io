About

Computational methods for historical meaning.

I am a doctoral researcher at the University of Helsinki, working at the intersection of natural language processing, computer vision, semantic matching, and digital humanities. My current research asks how models can be made more robust for historical evidence: data that is fragmented, multilingual, noisy, and shaped by changing social contexts.

Before Helsinki, I completed a master's degree in computer science at ShanghaiTech University, where I worked on vision-language reasoning and multimodal machine learning.

Research

Questions I return to

I

Semantic change at scale

Adapting computational methods to study how meaning shifts across historical corpora, genres, and social environments.

II

Robust semantic matching

Building matching and reasoning methods that remain useful when data is incomplete, heterogeneous, or historically distant.

III

Multimodal reasoning

Studying how language and vision models ground relations between texts, images, and structured evidence.

Project

CASCADE

I am part of CASCADE, an EU MSCA Doctoral Network on Computational Analysis of Semantic Change Across Different Environments. My PhD project focuses on adapting computational methods to challenges in historical research at scale.
